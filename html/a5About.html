Bgl Pal is a tool to track blood glucose levels, insulin doses, food intake, and exercise.

ALWAYS use your common sense and training from doctors for type 1 diabetes.

DON'T rely on the numbers being accurate: calculation (and coding) errors can happen.

Customisable to your tastes not everything has to be done all at once, it doesn't have to be in order, and it doesn't even have to be done to much detail - it will still 'work'

BG Unit mg/dL is milligrams per 0.1 litres. An adult has about 5 litres of blood, so 100mg/dL = about 5000mg of glucose in their blood. Which is about 1 teaspoon.

mmol/L is millimolar per litre. A millimole of glucose is about 180 mg. So 5.5 mmol/L = about 1000mg of glucose in 1 litre. In 5 litres, that's 1 teaspoon.

'Mole' and 'Millimole' are also good Pokemon names.
Threshold Define what you would like to flag as your hypo, target-bgl, and hyper range.
Flags You want to flag unusual patterns, not simply a 'normal' week. A flag rate of 1/5 means if you hit 1 hypo every 5 days in a particular "timezone" range, it'll flag it in the step chart (see Week Pattern).

But you might want to set the rate higher - because if everything is flagged as important, nothing stands out.
Insulin Name your insulins (eg 'Billie Jean') You don't have to use the official names. You can also hide unused insulin columns in Log Entry.

Some pens or pumps do more precision than whole units; you can set precision as 0.1, 0.5 or 1.
G.I. The glycemic index might be useful if you like getting into details. I like to know because I use medium and rapid insulin for different GI's. You can ignore it though.
Time 'zones' Different times of day might require different amounts of insulin. I use around 3-4 timezones, because I notice a difference between more insulin in the morning vs less in the evening.
Log times These times are pre-loaded into an empty Log Entry day. So you don't have to type 'em out yourself every time.

Part A: MODELLING
Timezone factors -> for 1 *season* timezones reflect that insulin needs/ratios can change throughout the day. Eg, a lunch donut might need three-quarters of the insulin a breakfast donut does.

Mmmm... donuts... what was I saying again?

All the calculations in Bgl Pal are indexed to timezones and seasons, so that graphs and data will be comparable, whatever time of day or season

But the most important thing is working these factors out to begin with, using the helpful graphs below to get a feel for realistic ratios.
Season factors seasons reflect that different seasons can mean different activities and different insulin needs.

Maybe for you, there isn't much change; maybe there is. You can set different/same factors in any case.

The factors will interpolate, meaning if (mid)Summer = 1.0 and (mid)Autumn = 1.2, then halfway between (endSummer/startAutumn) = 1.1.
Part B1: REFERENCE FOOD
details pick a food from the Food Database (or enter your own) to be your Reference/Index.

Pick one that has:
-enough sugar to test
-known amount of sugar
-pretty fast glycemic index (not pizza!)
-and you don't hate the taste of it

A box of cola cans is my choice -> this is then the reference/index.

Eg if a can of cola (35g sugar) raises my bgl +7 mmol/L at noon in Summer, then 35g/7mmol/L -> 1g glucose = +0.2 mmol/L for that timezone/season. This ratio of sugar to bgl is assumed to apply to all other foods in the same way at that same time/season.

Say a mysteriously-labelled food raises my bgl +14mmol/L, it probably has 70g glucose. This is all background calculation really - the main point of the reference food is to zero in on 1 particular food (it has the added benefit of working out 'mystery' foods' sugar content).

If I find (after a few noon tests) that I use 10U insulin to get zero bgl change, then 35g/10U -> 3.5g glucose = 1U insulin.

Also, 10U insulin covers a rise of +7 mmol/L, so 7mmol/L/10U -> 0.7 mmol/L = 1U insulin. +140 mg/dL at noon in Summer, then 35g/70mg/dL -> 1g glucose = +2 mg/dL for that timezone/season. This ratio of sugar to bgl is assumed to apply to all other foods in the same way at that same time/season.
